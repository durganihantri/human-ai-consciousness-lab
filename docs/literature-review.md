# Literature Matrix v0.1 — Perceived AI Consciousness

**Project:** Human–AI Consciousness Lab  
**Track:** 01 — Consciousness Attribution in Human–AI Interaction  
**Prepared:** 2026-11-23  
**Status:** Scoping evidence map; not a systematic review, meta-analysis, or preregistration.

## Scope and evidence standards

This matrix maps published scholarship onto the repository's existing research questions (RQ1–RQ5) and candidate hypotheses (H1–H3). The focus is **human judgments about AI**, not proof that AI has or lacks phenomenal consciousness. Entries were checked against publisher, PubMed, university repository, or scholarly bibliographic records. Most methodological summaries below are based on accessible abstracts and institutional descriptions rather than independent full-text appraisal; consequently, precise effect sizes, sampling details, scale reliability, and risk-of-bias ratings are **not verified** here. The study descriptions and limitations must not be read as a completed systematic evidence assessment.

**Legend:** E = empirical study; T = theory/review; M = methods/taxonomy; Q = qualitative; D = data descriptor. “Supports” indicates conceptual or indirect evidence, not confirmation of our exact hypotheses.

## Research-question alignment

- **RQ1:** How do conversational cues influence users' perceived AI consciousness?
- **RQ2:** How does first-person self-reference affect perceived agency and subjective experience?
- **RQ3:** How does apparent conversational continuity affect perceived identity and trust?
- **RQ4:** Does emotionally responsive language increase perceived understanding, separately from perceived consciousness?
- **RQ5:** How do prior beliefs and familiarity with AI moderate judgments?
- **H1:** First-person self-reference increases *perceived agency* versus matched neutral language.
- **H2:** Apparent cross-conversation continuity increases *perceived identity* versus discontinuity.
- **H3:** Empathetic wording increases *perceived emotional understanding*; effects on subjective-experience attribution remain exploratory.

## Evidence-to-hypothesis assessment

| Hypothesis / question | Current assessment | Reason and next action |
|---|---|---|
| **RQ1 — Conversational cues and perceived consciousness** | **Plausible, but underdetermined** | Social cues affect anthropomorphism/social presence (L01, L06–L08), but perceived *subjective experience* is a distinct outcome (L02). Use separate dependent variables. |
| **H1 — First-person self-reference → perceived agency** | **Indirectly motivated; not directly established** | Existing evidence on human-like cues does not isolate first-person pronouns. Manipulate first-person language while holding competence, warmth, task success, and wording length constant. |
| **H2 — Apparent memory continuity → perceived identity** | **Research gap in this matrix; no direct causal support verified** | Self-reported AI memory encounters (L10) do not test the proposed causal relationship. Use a controlled continuity manipulation; separate *apparent* memory from genuine retention. |
| **H3 — Empathetic wording → perceived emotional understanding** | **Plausible; no direct effect established by the selected sources** | Evidence about social presence, mind perception, and trust is related but not a clean test of this contrast. Pretest empathetic vs neutral scripts for warmth, clarity, and appropriateness. Keep effects on perceived experience exploratory. |
| **RQ5 — Prior beliefs / familiarity** | **Strong conceptual rationale; exact moderators unverified** | Anthropomorphism varies by person (L03–L05), and expectations differ (L13). Premeasure AI familiarity and baseline consciousness beliefs. |
| **RQ3 — Continuity and trust** | **Not established** | Trust has multiple drivers (L12). Distinguish continuity, reliability, privacy expectations, and trust; do not infer mediation without a planned design. |
| **RQ4 — Emotional responsiveness vs consciousness** | **Essential construct-separation question** | Separate ratings of warmth, perceived understanding, agency, and capacity for subjective experience (L02, L09). |

## Proposed operationalization (draft, not validated)

| Construct | Working definition | Candidate response item (original draft; requires validation) |
|---|---|---|
| Perceived agency | Attribution of goal-directed choice or control to the AI | “This AI seems capable of making goal-directed decisions.” |
| Perceived subjective experience | Attribution of felt, first-person experiences to the AI | “This AI seems capable of having subjective experiences.” |
| Perceived emotional understanding | Judgment that the AI understands the emotional meaning of a message | “This AI seems to understand the user's feelings.” |
| Perceived identity continuity | Judgment that the same AI identity persists across interactions | “This AI seems to have a continuous identity across conversations.” |
| Trust | Willingness to rely on the AI within a specified task | “I would rely on this AI for the task shown.” |
| Anthropomorphism | Attribution of humanlike characteristics to the AI | Measure separately from consciousness; assess validated instruments before selection. |

These are **illustrative items, not a psychometrically validated scale**. Do not average them into a single consciousness score without evidence of construct validity. Include manipulation checks and task-specific trust measures.

## Candidate design: controlled vignette pilot

1. **Materials:** Synthetic, non-clinical chatbot dialogues; no private transcripts or participant data.
2. **Manipulations:** (a) first-person vs impersonal self-reference; (b) apparent continuity vs no continuity; (c) empathetic vs neutral wording. Initially pilot **one manipulation at a time**; only expand to factorial designs after checking feasibility and statistical power.
3. **Outcomes:** Separate perceived agency, subjective experience, emotional understanding, identity continuity, social presence, and trust ratings.
4. **Controls:** Response quality, length, readability, task completion, explicit AI disclosure, model labeling, and emotional intensity.
5. **Moderators:** Prior beliefs about AI consciousness, familiarity with chatbots, individual differences in anthropomorphism; treat analyses as exploratory until preregistered.
6. **Bias checks:** Randomize vignette order; measure demand characteristics and manipulation recognition; include attention checks proportionate to the design.
7. **Ethics:** Only synthetic materials in technical development. Obtain applicable review/consent/data-management approval **before recruiting human participants**.
8. **Analysis:** Determine primary endpoint, sample size via power/simulation, exclusion rules, statistical model, multiplicity approach, and missing-data plan before confirmatory collection.

## Priority gaps for v0.2

- Search for **direct, recent LLM-era experiments** manipulating self-reference, apparent memory, and empathic wording; this matrix is foundational, not exhaustive.
- Verify full-text methods and effect estimates, including null findings and conflicting results.
- Identify and license validated scales for anthropomorphism, mind perception, social presence, and trust; test construct separation.
- Include cross-cultural and multilingual studies; consider English/Mandarin replication rather than assuming measurement invariance.
- Define systematic search databases, inclusion/exclusion criteria, search strings, screening log, and appraisal method if upgrading to a systematic review.
- Distinguish direct human–AI interaction from AI-mediated human–human communication.
